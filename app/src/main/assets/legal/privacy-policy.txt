Streaming Radio privacy policy
Updated 11 November 2026. Publisher brand: Impostor Labs.
Contact: impostor.labs@gmail.com

This policy describes Streaming Radio for Android and Android Automotive OS.

The app has no user sign-in or advertising SDK. It stores playlist
URLs, last-good playlist contents, station order, Favorites, stream selections,
settings and chosen artwork on your device. Automotive backup is disabled.
Uninstalling or clearing app storage removes that app-private data. Removing a
playlist does not remove saved Favorites or all cached artwork.

Playlist refreshes and radio playback connect directly to your chosen hosts.
Those hosts receive your IP address and ordinary request information. Do not
put passwords or private tokens in public playlists. Release playlist/catalog
and artwork requests use HTTPS. Some user-selected radio streams may use HTTP;
HTTP playback is not encrypted. The app does not operate those stream hosts.

Online artwork is enabled by default unless a saved preference turns it off.
When enabled, station-name queries go to Radio Browser and artist/title queries
may go to MusicBrainz and Cover Art Archive/image hosts. Each recipient sees
your request and IP address and applies its own privacy policy. User-entered
image search terms are sent to the station directory. Cached images are local.
The local debug prototype may also use Apple/iTunes Search; that integration
is disabled in the public candidate pending its promotional-content terms.

Optional playlist transfer: When configured and used, this feature uses
Firebase Authentication and Realtime Database to pair the phone and car.
Firebase receives anonymous technical account identifiers, connection
information such as your IP address, and the playlist URL or M3U contents
and optional name you submit. HTTPS protects the transfer in transit. The
playlist contents are not end-to-end encrypted. Manual playlist entry
and radio playback do not require Firebase. The bundled default configuration
keeps playlist transfer disabled until a Firebase project is configured.

Pairing access expires after five minutes. The app tries to delete completed
or cancelled transfers and retries cleanup when a client returns. Expiration
blocks access but does not delete the data. If cleanup never succeeds, the
server records may remain indefinitely. The phone browser and car may keep
technical credentials and pending session details locally to retry cleanup.
Anonymous Firebase accounts do not have a guaranteed deletion deadline.

Google Cast is available only in the phone app. Its SDK may perform device
discovery while the app is in use, as well as when you start a Cast session. It
sends encrypted, anonymous Cast interaction and app activity data to Google,
including generic discovery and session events and mobile-device/client-app information.
Google says this information is not tied to an individual user, is used in
aggregate to improve Cast, and is retained briefly before aggregation and
deletion. This SDK collection cannot be disabled by the app or user, and the
app developer cannot request deletion of those SDK records. The SDK does not
send this telemetry to other apps or unrelated third parties. Google handles
its own services under its privacy policy. Casting also sends the selected
station's stream URL, name, station identifier and, if available, genre to
the Cast receiver. The receiver fetches audio directly from the station
host, which receives the receiver's ordinary network request
information. Avoid casting streams whose URLs contain private credentials.

Turning off automatic artwork stops new artwork/catalog requests; it does not
stop playlist retrieval or playback you request. Requests already in progress
may finish. Track-image cache is bounded; selected station images remain until
replaced, removed by cache maintenance, or app data is cleared.

Optional diagnostic logs stay on the device. They may contain station/track
metadata, URLs and controller details. Public builds do not enable drive traces
by default. Do not share logs without reviewing them for private information.
